Edward Hunter

Bio Excerpt: "He was born of a respectable and prosperous family in Pennysylvania. From his youth he was upright in his dealings, honest and straightforward, candid even to bluntness in speech, yet he possessed a heart overflowing with kindness. Childlike and humble, he was nevertheless astute and discerning, charitable to all. Known to be an exhorter of faithfulness, he urged all to strive to be obedient in all things. His integrity and devotion became well known to all people who knew him."

Pivotal Moment Excerpt: "In the spring of 1839, opposition arose against the strangers called 'Mormons,' Edward Hunter recalled the condition he gave to use the seminary building that he fully funded to be rebuilt, that all might worship freely in the building he reestablished. Hearing of plans to harm these Mormon Missionaries while preaching their message in Locust Groce, he mounted his horse and rode out alone. When this missionary was ordered to stop, Edward stood among the crowd and declared that this stranger should have justice and be heard, resolving to protect him even at the risk of his own life."
